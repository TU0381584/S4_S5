S1. Overview
This supplement gives the detail behind the main paper’s verification runs and additional results for
AXIOM, short for Adaptive Cross-cell Integrated Orchestration of Multi-slice admission. Every pass
criterion was fixed in a pre-registration committed before the run it judged. The baselines are a single-
agent deep Q-network (DQN-SA), independent per-gNB deep Q-networks (DQN-I), and multi-agent
proximal policy optimisation (MAPPO), where DQN denotes a deep Q-network in general. AXIOM’s
encoder is a graph attention network (GAT), and its variant with a per-node multi-layer perceptron
(MLP) encoder is called the MLP variant. Table S1 lists the other acronyms used in this supplement.

S2. Verification runs
We reran eleven reported settings on their original seeds in fresh processes, and each reproduced its
stored per-seed results exactly, as did the Env A static rule, which sheds every mMTC request, on all
30 seeds. Sixteen runs that no earlier rerun covered matched their stored per-seed results exactly, in
Env A at seven, nineteen, and thirty-seven gNBs and in Env B at seven gNBs. DQN-SA is the one
exception, because its training code builds the network before it sets the seed. Dropping the affected
seeds changes no comparison’s direction. With its initialisation seeded, DQN-SA then reproduced exactly
across processes.
    Four further runs retrained every policy on seeds from 3000 onwards, never used before, and 81 of their
90 checks stated in advance held. In Env A AXIOM reached 98.2% of the static rule’s reward at three
gNBs and at nineteen gNBs kept 95.6% priority-slice compliance against 47.8% to 65.5% for the baselines.
At seven gNBs on the hex layout and at nineteen on the fully-connected layout AXIOM kept 99.0% and
95.7% priority-slice compliance and beat every baseline. Federation again showed no significant cost, at
+0.077 with 95% CI [−1.03, 1.27]. Fresh seeds therefore confirmed the main findings of the paper. At

baseline under every layout.
    Five of the nine failed checks concern the baselines rather than AXIOM. On fresh seeds at seven gNBs
DQN-I and DQN-SA kept up to 83.3% priority-slice compliance, above the 80% bound we had set. The

S3. Live testbed and deployment check

without a near-real-time RAN Intelligent Controller (RIC), because the ORANSlice fork applies slicing
control directly through a lightweight User Datagram Protocol (UDP) E2 agent. AXIOM itself is not
deployed on the live testbed. Its multi-gNB policy needs at least two live gNBs, and our two-gNB attempt
registered both with the core but did not sustain traffic. The testbed therefore validates the pipeline
AXIOM would use, namely the control path over the O-RAN E2 interface, PRB-ratio actuation, and
real-time inference. It does so with a single-agent DQN checkpoint trained offline for this rig, at one and

S4. Setup, safeguards, and analysis plans
Algorithm 1 of the main paper states AXIOM’s training loop, and its parameter table lists the simulation
and training settings.
    A sensitivity study repeats the learned policies and the static rule in Env A on 10 seeds at 102% and
107% of the nominal load. It also changes the slice weights, lowering eMBB from 3.5 to 2.5 and raising
mMTC from 0.3 to 0.6. A second repeats AXIOM in Env B on 10 seeds at learning rates of 3×10−4 and
3×10−3 and with an encoder twice as wide. The federated campaign runs in Env A on seeds 900 to 909,
with noise multipliers σ ∈ {0, 0.5, 1, 2, 4} and a replication on seeds 1000 to 1009. Each round runs 50
local steps of federated averaging (FedAvg) with a gradient-clip norm of 1.0.
    A validation screen checks every trained checkpoint of every learned policy before evaluation. Each
plays five greedy episodes on its own training stream and fails if it blocks no request or if fewer than 90%
of its blocks target mMTC. Every seed is reported whether or not it passes. In Env B the reward-optimal
policy also sheds eMBB, so an amendment recorded before any result was read makes the no-blocking
criterion the primary screen there. The two Env B constants were calibrated on pilot seeds with rules
only. The main pre-registration fixed them together with AXIOM’s learning rate and skip connection,
all chosen on pilot seeds 2000 to 2009.
    AXIOM’s encoder is a graph-attention network over the gNB graph. At every control step each gNB
forms its own feature row xi of PRB-used ratio, congestion level, and normalised queue length. A two-
layer network with H=4 heads then produces one embedding per gNB. Each head h uses a learned source
vector ash and destination vector adh on the shared projection Wh . Here LeakyReLU, the leaky rectified
linear unit, has slope 0.2 for negative inputs, and Ni is the set of neighbours of node i,

Every agent’s loss updates this shared encoder, so training sees the full cluster state. At execution
each agent reads only its own row zi to decide its pending requests. An earlier variant mixed each gNB’s
summed per-request values with a QMIX-style monotonic mixer. Its target changed scale with the request
count, and its mean training loss diverged from about 76 to about 409. Treating each request as its own
sample removed that dependence.
    In the federated variant each client’s encoder still reads the full joint node-feature snapshot. Differen-
tial privacy bounds how much any one client’s training data can change what the shared model reveals.
Each client applies a differentially private stochastic gradient descent (DP-SGD) step, clipping its mini-
batch gradient to norm Cdp and adding Gaussian noise of standard deviation σCdp . Zero-concentrated
differential privacy (zCDP) composes this over the m noisy steps a client takes, with ε and δ the usual
privacy-loss and failure-probability parameters,

We credit no amplification by subsampling, because minibatches come from a replay buffer that reuses
records, so the bound is conservative. The noise protects each client’s admission decisions and rewards,
but not the congestion features that every encoder reads by design.
    The trivial-policy check evaluates the simplest rules before trusting any learned policy. In our first
simulator every slice was served independently against its own ceiling. There, accepting every request
earned 14.45 reward per step and kept URLLC and eMBB at 100% SLA compliance, more than any
learned policy, so admission control had nothing to do. The shared-budget simulator of the main paper
removes that loophole. Over the same 30 held-out seeds, accepting every request now earns 9.89 reward
per step and lets eMBB compliance fall to 56.8%, whereas shedding every mMTC request earns 12.84
and keeps URLLC and eMBB at 100%. Replication on a disjoint seed sample caught one artefact. Queue
backlog carried over between episodes, so one early transient spoiled every later episode for every policy,
the static rule included. Every result in this paper comes from the corrected simulator, which empties
all queues at the start of each episode.
    Each analysis plan was committed to the project’s version-control history before the runs it governs,
with one exception. The main campaign reuses Env A results of DQN-SA, DQN-I, and the static rule
from an earlier rerun, M63. They cover three gNBs on the primary and replication seeds, the sensitivity
study, seven gNBs, and 15 of the 30 seeds at nineteen gNBs. The M63 plan was committed one minute
after that rerun was launched and before any of its results were read. Two development stages are
also disclosed, although neither changed a reported result. A protocol, M67, tested checkpoint selection
and both encoders on new seeds, found no decisive gain over DQN-SA, and left AXIOM unchanged.
Exploratory probes on seeds 900 to 904 and pilot seeds 2000 to 2004 suggested the load-shift effect of
Section S7 and the hindsight bound of the main paper’s Env B results before their reported runs. Three
figures describe stored runs after the results were known, with no plan or test of their own. The main
paper maps per-gNB compliance at nineteen gNBs. Section S2 sets reported beside fresh-seed differences,
and Section S7 shows AXIOM’s learned eMBB shedding after replaying every stored evaluation exactly.
    An artificial intelligence (AI) assistant, Anthropic’s Opus agent, was used to help write the analysis
and plotting scripts and to edit the manuscript text. The authors made all research decisions and take
full responsibility for the content of this paper.


S5. Three gNBs, ablations, federation, and disruption
The MLP variant reads only a gNB’s own features. In Env A it ties AXIOM, which earns 0.058 more
reward per step at three gNBs with 95% CI [−0.17, 0.26] and 0.31 more at nineteen gNBs on the ring
layout, with 7 of 12 seeds tied. In Env B it earns slightly more, 0.116 per step at three gNBs in 17 of 30
seeds at p = 0.45. At seven gNBs it earns 1.12 and 0.95 more, but neither lead survives Holm correction.
On ten fresh seeds at seven gNBs in Env B, however, it led by 1.38 reward per step, in 9 of 10 seeds at
p = 0.006.
    A disjoint replication on seeds 1000 to 1009 is stronger for AXIOM. There it earns 1.083, 0.799, and
3.806 more reward per step than DQN-SA, DQN-I, and MAPPO. It sits only 0.018 below the static rule
on those seeds. Pooled over all 40 seeds its margins over the three baselines are 0.504, 1.032, and 2.965
reward per step at Holm-adjusted p ≤ 0.002. Fresh seeds in Section S2 do not reproduce the lead over
DQN-SA, however. In Env B at three gNBs the disjoint ten-seed replication points the same way, with
AXIOM winning 7 or 8 of 10 seeds against each, but no lead survives correction there.

Env A at three gNBs and 99.96% priority-slice compliance at nineteen. On 60 further fresh seeds across
four settings it was again never significantly different from AXIOM, ahead in three settings and behind

S6. Scaling to thirty-seven gNBs and stronger baselines
In the gate map of the main paper’s thirty-seven-gNB cluster, DQN-SA leaves every admission gate open
at 0.93 to 0.95 and DQN-I closes the mMTC gate only to 0.94. MAPPO there partly closes the eMBB
gate to 0.80 but leaves mMTC open at 0.51. At seven gNBs AXIOM keeps URLLC and eMBB at 99.8%
and 94.4%, against 91.4% and 61.0% for DQN-SA, 94.2% and 55.0% for DQN-I, and 87.0% and 48.4% for
MAPPO. At nineteen gNBs it keeps them at 99.4% and 95.1%, and at thirty-seven at 96.7% and 90.7%.
    The thirty-seven-gNB configuration extends the nineteen-gNB one exactly as the seven- and nineteen-
gNB configurations extended three, with 37 arrivals per step and at most 148 pending requests. AXIOM
ran 30 seeds on the hex layout and 15 on each of the ring and fully-connected layouts. In Env A the
baselines and the static rule never read the layout, so each ran once on the 30 hex seeds, and Table S2
lists every policy.
    AXIOM’s six collapsed runs keep 59% to 65% priority-slice compliance, while its other runs keep
96.9% to 98.1% on average under each layout. We also ran MAPPO with the configuration that the main
paper’s grid selected for Env A, trained for 900 episodes. It keeps 65.6% priority-slice compliance and
151.90 reward per step and never collapses. AXIOM beats it by 29.2 points in 13 of 15 seeds at p < 0.001
and by 23.23 reward per step in all 15.
    The fresh-seed replication retrained every policy on seeds 6100 to 6119 on the hex layout, as the
lower part of Table S2 lists. AXIOM leads DQN-SA by 37.7 points of priority-slice compliance and 35.46
reward per step, DQN-I by 61.0 and 25.62, and MAPPO by 36.5 and 30.63. It wins 18 to 19 of 20 seeds
in every comparison, all at Holm-adjusted p < 0.001. Its one collapsed seed is 6110, and pooled with
the 30 hex seeds above, AXIOM keeps 94.2% priority-slice compliance over 50 seeds and collapses in 4 of
them.
    Accepting every request is the floor that any admission controller should clear. In Env A it earns
26.88, 78.55, and 156.22 reward per step at seven, nineteen, and thirty-seven gNBs. At nineteen and
thirty-seven gNBs every baseline keeps less priority-slice compliance than accept-all on average, and
DQN-SA’s runs that do not collapse keep only 40.0% and 35.9%, DQN-I’s 55.9% and 29.1%.

S7. Env B, shifted load, longer training, and convergence
No deployable policy we tested keeps eMBB compliance above 37% in Env B, because demand at the
load peaks exceeds the budget by up to 23.5%. Even the rule with the highest eMBB compliance among
all 95 local rules, chosen in hindsight on each evaluation stream, reaches only 37.7% on average. The
best rule by reward in hindsight earns only 0.14% more than the tuned rule.
    Table S5 lists AXIOM against the rule tuned on nominal conditions, on 25 checkpoints neither re-
trained nor re-tuned. A rule re-tuned on the shifted training streams selected thresholds different from
the nominal rule’s at every shift. Against it AXIOM trails by 0.16 and 0.12 per step at 110% and 115%
load, which is not significant, and by 0.62 at 120% load. Under 110% to 120% load AXIOM beats the
nominally tuned rule in 21 to 23 of 25 seeds at Holm-adjusted p ≤ 0.0002. At seven gNBs on the ring
layout the reversal is larger. AXIOM trails the rule by 3.84 reward per step under nominal load but leads
it by 1.86 and 3.14 at 110% and 120% load.

S9. Reproducing the results
Fig. S7 sets out what reproducing the results needs and the order of the steps. Every result in both
documents comes from one laptop without a graphics processor, and every simulation run fixes its seed.
Reruns on the same seeds reproduce the stored per-seed results exactly, except the original DQN-SA
runs, whose initial weights were not seeded, as Section S2 explains. The code, configurations, and stored
logs are available from the corresponding author on reasonable request.

  1   Set up. Request the code, configurations, and stored logs from the corresponding author. framework/ holds the
      simulators and learners, experiments/scripts/ the campaign scripts, experiments/configs/ the cluster configu-
      rations, experiments/results/ the stored per-seed logs, and docs/ the pre-registrations. Create a Python virtual
      environment with the packages above. Every job runs from framework/ on one thread, with OMP_NUM_THREADS=1,
      MKL_NUM_THREADS=1, and OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1.

  2   Train and evaluate. One command trains and evaluates one policy on a batch of seeds, for example
      M63_BACKLOG_RESET=1 M64_AXIOM_V2=1 python m64_run.py --arm axiom2 --env A --n 19
         --topology ring --seeds 900 901 902 903 904 --out-dir ../experiments/results/m65_scaling/n19_ring
      The arms are axiom2, single_agent_dqn, independent_dqn, mappo_tuned, and the rules, whose thresholds are
      chosen on training streams. Each seed trains for 300 episodes on its own stream and is then evaluated for 50
      episodes on the held-out stream 5000 plus the seed. Each seed writes a training log, a checkpoint, and an evalu-
      ation log, and each batch a JSON file of reward, blocks, and training time. The median training time per seed runs
      from 73 s to 2,088 s, and the main campaigns ran five jobs at a time. The primary seeds are 900 to 929, the repli-
      cation 1000 to 1009, the pilots 2000 to 2009, and the fresh seeds 3000 onwards.


  3   Run the other campaigns. m3_run_experiment.py, called through m62_shared_env.py, trains the federated vari-
      ant with noise multipliers 0, 0.5, 1, 2, and 4. m4_run_experiment.py evaluates frozen checkpoints under dropout,
      spikes, and churn. m73_run.py adds the 37-gNB configuration, and m70_run.py, m75_run.py, and m76_run.py
      add the follow-up variants, selected by environment variables. m63_validation_screen.py screens every trained
      checkpoint on its own training stream.


  4   Re-evaluate stored checkpoints. m72_shift.py eval replays AXIOM and the nominally tuned rule at 80% to
      120% load, stronger coupling, and a wider load swing, and m72_shift.py retune re-tunes the rule on shifted train-
      ing streams. m77_kpi.py records the PRBs served and the backlog, and confirms that each re-run reproduces its
      stored log. m81_decision_probe.py replays every stored evaluation to read what the eMBB head decides.


  5   Summarise. m65_rebuild_all.sh runs m64_analysis.py and m62_analysis.py over the main campaigns, and
      each later campaign has its own analysis script. Each writes the summaries and judges every pre-registered cri-
      terion in docs/PAPER5_M*_preregistration.md, reporting each whether it holds or fails.


  6   Verify. m66_verify.py --tex main.tex --supp supplementary.tex reads the raw logs with its own reader and
      Holm correction, and recomputes every results table and key sentence of both documents. Its m82_audit.py traces
      every remaining number to the configurations and the code. m66_ci_recompute.py recomputes every bootstrap CI
