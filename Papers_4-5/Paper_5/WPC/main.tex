\title[GAT-CTDE for Federated Slice Admission Control]{GAT-CTDE: A Correctness-Aware Graph-Attention Architecture for Federated Multi-Agent Slice Admission Control}

\author[1]{\fnm{Manoj Prasad} \sur{Kunasegran}}\nomail

\author*[1]{\fnm{Wai Leong} \sur{Pang}}\email{waileong.pang@taylors.edu.my}

\author[1]{\fnm{Swee King} \sur{Phang}}\nomail

\affil*[1]{\orgdiv{School of Engineering}, \orgname{Taylor's University}, \orgaddress{\city{Subang Jaya}, \country{Malaysia}}}

\abstract{This paper delivers a working realisation of a graph-attention, multi-agent, federated slice admission controller, GAT-CTDE, treating correctness-aware evaluation as a the primary contribution alongside the architecture. This realisation closed three research gaps identified from our prior systematic review. On the metric that reflects the actual training objective, GAT-CTDE decisively beats an independent-learner ablation, though its edge over a single-agent baseline is small and not statistically decisive at this sample size. Scaled from 3 to 19 gNodeBs (gNBs) under a focused stress regime, GAT-CTDE resists a training collapse, demonstrating its advantage over the single-agent baseline, which completely collapsed. The GAT-CTDE model converged across 64 independently-drawn seeds on a residual 35.4\% [25.5\%, 45.8\%] collapse rate already present at 7 gNBs. Additionally, privacy-preserving federated learning costs nothing measurable in reward and differential privacy degrades decision precision only past a real threshold, while disruption cost accelerates with severity for every arm. Two safeguards make these results trustworthy, namely a correctness-aware metric pair (44 of 49 independent checks disagreeing with the standard compliance metric) and a reproduction-versus-replication discipline. A live single-gNB deployment confirms the offline-trained decision logic executes correctly on real OpenAirInterface (OAI) hardware. During cross-layer integrity checks, a scheduler-configuration demonstrated that this rig cannot reach genuine per-slice scarcity at any tested load, so the live result establishes deployability rather than admission-control behaviour under contention, tested instead in the disclosed offline stress regime.}

\keywords{Network Slicing, Slice Admission Control, Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning, Graph Attention Networks, Federated Learning, Open RAN}

\section{Introduction}
Network slicing lets a single physical radio access network serve heterogeneous service classes, namely Ultra-Reliable Low-Latency Communication (URLLC), enhanced Mobile Broadband (eMBB), and massive Machine-Type Communication (mMTC), under distinct per-slice Service Level Agreements (SLAs). Our earlier work~\cite{mecon-paper1,icraie-paper2}
established that a Deep Q-Network (DQN) admission controller can learn
to enforce these SLAs under a single gNodeB (gNB), validated on a live
OpenAirInterface (OAI) testbed. A real multi-cell deployment does not admit requests at one gNB in isolation, since neighbouring cells share backhaul, spectrum planning, and a cluster-wide load-balance objective, making coordinated admission a genuinely multi-agent problem. This necessitates the investigation of a shared, graph-structured representation of the cluster's state to determine whether a set of per-gNB agents make better joint decisions than a single agent using a flattened joint state, or independent per-gNB agents with no shared representation. This research question motivated the premise for this paper.
Fig.~\ref{fig:sysview} places this setting in context.

This paper's contribution is GAT-CTDE, a centralised-training,
decentralised-execution multi-agent architecture built on a shared
Graph Attention Network (GAT)~\cite{gat} encoder over the gNB topology
feeding decentralised per-slice Q-heads, delivered together with the
evaluation discipline needed to trust what it reports. Three gaps
motivate this pairing, each named directly against our own systematic
review of 67 core studies (Section~\ref{sec:litreview}). First, no
reviewed study checks whether its own outcome metric can score a
degenerate policy no worse than a correct one. Second, none replicates
a headline architectural finding on an independent seed sample before
reporting it. Third, none tests whether a live testbed can actually
reach the contention regime its offline claims are evaluated under. We
close all three gaps, beginning with a per-component realisation of the architecture (GAT
encoder, Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) controller,
Federated Learning (FL) coordinator, Differential Privacy (DP) layer),
each with its own
measured, isolated contribution rather than one end-to-end number, a
correctness-aware metric pair and reproduction-versus-replication
discipline applied throughout rather than asserted once, and a live
single-gNB deployment that tests, rather than assumes, whether this
rig's own achievable load ever produces genuine per-slice scarcity.

Extended to federated training with differential privacy and evaluated under mid-episode gNB dropout, demand spikes, and agent churn, we scale the architecture to cluster sizes $N \in \{7, 19\}$ under ring and hex-grid adjacency. It resists a training collapse that becomes total for a flattened-state single-agent baseline, converging on a residual 35.4\% collapse rate across 64 independently-drawn seeds rather than either of two disagreeing small-sample estimates, a pattern already present at $N{=}7$ rather than only emerging at large cluster size. On the metric that reflects the actual training objective, the architecture decisively beats an independent-learner ablation that isolates the same coordination contribution, though its edge over the single-agent baseline specifically is small and not statistically decisive at this sample size, an honest limit we report. Federating the architecture costs nothing
measurable in reward, and differential privacy degrades decision
precision only past a real threshold rather than a smooth trade-off.
Under disruption, its cost accelerates with severity like every arm
tested, with a coordination-specific penalty at only the mildest
severity tested.

These safeguards are not abstract,  The correctness-aware pair
diagnosed and fixed a real training collapse our first encoder
suffered on all 30 campaign seeds (Section~\ref{sec:methodology-perslice}), and the
reproduction-versus-replication discipline caught and retracted a real
architectural overclaim under mid-episode disruption (Section~\ref{sec:results-m4}). The live deployment below both
confirms the offline-trained decision logic executes correctly on real
hardware and also uncovered a cross-layer scheduler misconfiguration that had
earlier manufactured the appearance of live contention. This was fixed at its
root, after which we noted that the rig cannot exercise genuine per-slice scarcity
at any load level tested, confining the disruption and scaling claims
above to the disclosed offline stress regime where they are made.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:litreview}
Our prior systematic review~\cite{ref:access} screened 6{,}286 records down to 67 core 2024--2026 studies on machine-learning-driven, user-centric distributed network slicing, organised around three enabling pillars, namely topology-aware graph representation, distributed multi-agent control, and privacy-preserving federation, and found individual pillars well developed but rarely combined under realistic constraints, and never with a validated live deployment. None of the works we reviewed, there or here, checks whether its own evaluation metric can mask a degenerate policy, or replicates a headline finding on an independent seed sample. This is the gap this paper fills.

The closest prior works run both checks against a cluster of three groups. Shao et al.~\cite{shao2021gatmarl} (the precedent behind our own GAT encoder choice, showing it strengthens inter-agent cooperation over plain DQN/Advantage Actor-Critic (A2C) baselines), Ahmadi et al.~\cite{ahmadi2024generalizable} (topology generalisation via graph attention, complementary to, not answered by, the fixed-topology evaluation-integrity question we ask in Section~\ref{sec:results-m6}), and Sulaiman et al.~\cite{sulaiman2023coordinated} (the closest prior art to our own joint slicing-and-admission-control problem) together check neither whether their own reward-optimal policy could be mistaken for a degenerate one nor a headline result against an independent seed sample. Tashman and Cherkaoui~\cite{tashman2026adversarial} and Fatehi et al.~\cite{fatehi2026attentionmarl} study robustness to a disruptive condition, as our own disruption campaign (Section~\ref{sec:results-m4}) does, but neither checks whether the metric reporting that robustness is itself trustworthy or replicates a finding on an independent sample, exactly the gap our own campaign's independent-seed replication closes, and not hypothetically, since it is what overturned our initial churn-immunity reading. Zhang et al.~\cite{zhang2022federatedoran} (the closest periodic-synchronisation precedent) and Yasin et al.~\cite{yasin2025dpfl} (the closest FL+DP precedent, targeting a different threat model, securing the controller rather than characterising a control policy's own privacy-utility trade-off, which our own $\sigma$-sweep in Section~\ref{sec:results-m3} reports) both lack a correctness-aware check on whether federating or privatising changed decision quality. A further, closely related set~\cite{mohajer2026pronto,bouroudi2025gnnmarl,qasim2025tgnnfl,gong2026femaddpg} combines two of these three pillars but, like every work above, evaluates exclusively on its own reported outcome metric, computed once, on one seed or run. This is the gap this paper fills, not a new point in the GAT/MARL/FL design space but a demonstration (on GAT-CTDE, extended to federation, privacy, and disruption) that checking a metric's own trustworthiness and replicating a finding on an independent sample are both necessary, and that skipping either changes what a reader is entitled to believe the resulting numbers mean.

We consider a cluster of $N{=}3$ gNBs, each serving the same three
slices URLLC, eMBB, and mMTC under a shared per-gNB Physical Resource
Block (PRB) budget $B$. An admission decision is realised as a
PRB-ceiling adjustment through the \texttt{slicing\_control\_m} min and
max ratio rather than a literal per-flow accept or reject, since the
real OAI xApp control API exposes only ratio ceilings. No real
multi-gNB physical topology is available to us, so we take the least
assumption-laden choice and treat the cluster as fully connected,
noting this as a modelling choice rather than a measured fact. At each
control step every gNB has zero or more pending admission requests,
each tagged with a slice identity, and an agent decides accept or
reject for each independently, conditioned on the current cluster
state. The discrete action space matches our single-gNB work exactly at
\texttt{action\_dim}${=}2$, so any coordination benefit found here is
attributable to the architecture and not a richer action space.

The step reward follows our prior single-gNB formulation, a per-slice
service term net of SLA-violation penalties, a congestion charge on
admitted volume, and a load-imbalance penalty across gNBs. The step
reward $r_t$ sums over slices indexed by $k$, with priority weight
$\omega_k$, per-accept reward $R_k$, admitted count $n_k$, violation
penalty $\lambda_k$, and the indicator $\mathbf{1}[\cdot]$ equal to one
exactly when slice $k$ is in SLA violation that step,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:reward}
r_t = \sum_{k}\!\big(\omega_k R_k\, n_k - \lambda_k\,\mathbf{1}[v_k]\big)
      \;-\; \kappa\, C_t \lVert a_t \rVert_1
      \;-\; \beta\,(1 - F_t),
\end{equation}
where $\lVert a_t \rVert_1$ is the total admitted this step, $C_t$ the
cluster-mean congestion level, $\kappa$ the congestion coefficient,
$F_t\!\in\![0,1]$ the min-over-max PRB-utilisation fairness ratio, and
$\beta$ the load-balance weight. The violation flag $v_k$ derives from a
continuous SLA margin $m_k$ that the correctness-aware metrics of
Section~\ref{sec:collapse} also build on, taken as the worse of the queue
and loss channels,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:margin}
m_k = \min\!\Big(1 - \tilde q_k,\; 1 - \tfrac{\ell_k}{\ell_k^{\max}}\Big),
\qquad v_k \Leftrightarrow m_k \le 0,
\end{equation}
with $\tilde q_k$ the slice backlog relative to its budget and
$\ell_k,\ell_k^{\max}$ its loss proxy and loss budget. These weights are
tuned in prior work so that the reward-optimal policy under congestion is
differentiated shedding, namely continue accepting URLLC, mostly accept
eMBB, and reject mMTC, confirmed by Q-value inspection on a working
baseline policy in Section~\ref{sec:methodology-perslice} rather than
assumed. Every result
except Section~\ref{sec:results-m8}'s bounded live anchor is obtained in
an offline, closed-loop simulation deliberately oversubscribed to
roughly 110\% of one gNB's PRB budget, reusing our existing multi-gNB
configuration unmodified. We use this offline environment explicitly as
a deliberately harder, disclosed stress environment for a contention
regime the live rig cannot currently produce, since at every native
load tested, one and two User Equipments (UEs) per slice alike, all
three slices serve essentially 100\% of offered demand with zero loss
in Section~\ref{sec:results-m8}. Multi-gNB and disruption claims
therefore remain offline-only. The concrete rig underlying every live
measurement reported later is the single-host, software-radio, RIC-free
O-RAN stack of Fig.~\ref{fig:testbed}, which the live deployment of
Section~\ref{sec:results-m8} exercises end to end.

\subsection{Shared Encoder, Decentralised Q-Heads}
\label{sec:methodology-perslice}
Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}a shows the architecture. At every control
step, each gNB's own per-slice features, namely PRB-used-ratio,
congestion-level, and queue-length-norm, form that node's row $x_i$ in a
joint node-feature matrix $X \in \mathbb{R}^{N\times d}$ over the $N$ gNB
nodes with $d$ features each. A two-layer, $H{=}4$-head GAT~\cite{gat}
produces one embedding per gNB. Rather than scoring a concatenation, each
head $h$ decomposes additive attention into a learned source and
destination vector $a_s^{h}, a_d^{h}$ applied to the shared linear
projection $W^{h}$, with $\phi$ the leaky rectifier of slope $0.2$ and
$\mathcal{N}_i$ the neighbours of node $i$ under the adjacency mask,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gat}
\varepsilon_{ij}^{h}=\phi\!\big(a_s^{h\top} W^{h} x_i + a_d^{h\top} W^{h} x_j\big),
\qquad
\alpha_{ij}^{h}=\frac{\exp\varepsilon_{ij}^{h}}{\sum_{l\in\mathcal{N}_i}\exp\varepsilon_{il}^{h}}.
\end{equation}
Here $\varepsilon_{ij}^{h}$ is the unnormalised attention score from node
$i$ to neighbour $j$ in head $h$, and $\alpha_{ij}^{h}$ its normalisation
over $i$'s neighbours, with $l$ ranging over $\mathcal{N}_i$. Hidden
layers concatenate the $H$ heads under an ELU nonlinearity, while the
final layer averages them, and a per-layer normalisation operator
$\mathrm{LN}(\cdot)$ that rescales each node's embedding to zero mean and
unit variance produces the final per-gNB embedding $z_i$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gat-agg}
z_i=\mathrm{LN}\!\Big(\tfrac{1}{H}\textstyle\sum_{h=1}^{H}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_i}\alpha_{ij}^{h}\,W^{h} x_j\Big).
\end{equation}
Centralisation comes from this encoder, whose parameters are updated by
every agent's loss jointly so that it sees the full cluster state during
training. Decentralised execution comes next, since at action-selection
time each agent consumes only its own embedding row $z_i$ to decide its
own pending requests.

Both the \texttt{LayerNorm} and the per-slice heads below were made
necessary by a real training collapse rather than added on theoretical
grounds. An early design fed each embedding, concatenated with a one-hot
slice context, into a single shared Q-head multi-layer perceptron. With
all three slices updating the same shared parameters, URLLC's large
priority weight produces proportionally large gradients that overwrite
mMTC's much smaller true signal, a standard shared-parameter class
imbalance that drove the evaluation policy to block zero requests.
Replacing the single shared head with one small perceptron per slice,
selected by the context one-hot, gives each slice its own parameters so
one slice's gradients can no longer overwrite another's. Together with
the \texttt{LayerNorm}, this reduced the collapse from 30 of 30 to 9 of
30 seeds, with correctly-targeted differentiated shedding rising from 0
to 21 of the 30 seeds. A seed here is one independent random
initialisation of network weights and environment stream, and we report
every seed rather than a single best run.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:gat-ctde} states the training loop. The encoder is
updated from every request in a sampled minibatch regardless of gNB or
slice, which is the centralised training, while action selection at
rollout time consults only the acting agent's own embedding row, which
is the decentralised execution. Every pending request at gNB $i$, slice
$k$ is its own temporal-difference sample, never summed across a step
before the loss. For a request with immediate reward $r$, next-state
embedding $z_i'$, and candidate next action $a'$, the bootstrapped target
$y$ at discount $\gamma$ and the minibatch loss are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:td}
y = r + \gamma\,(1-d)\max_{a'} Q_{\phi_k^-}\!\big(z_i'\big)[a'],
\qquad
\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{|B|}\sum_{(i,k,a)\in B}\ell_\delta\!\Big(Q_{\phi_k}(z_i)[a]-y\Big),
\end{equation}
where $Q_{\phi_k}$ is slice $k$'s online Q-head and $Q_{\phi_k^-}$ its
target copy, $d$ the episode-done flag, $B$ the sampled minibatch of
requests, and $\ell_\delta$ the Huber loss, used in place of squared
error to keep the deeper encoder stable under long bootstraps.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{GAT-CTDE: centralised training, decentralised execution}
\label{alg:gat-ctde}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE encoder $f_\theta$, per-slice Q-heads $\{Q_{\phi_k}\}$, targets $f_{\theta^-},\{Q_{\phi_k^-}\}$, buffer $\mathcal{D}$, adjacency $A$, discount $\gamma$, sync period $C$
\FOR{each step $t$, observing joint node features $X_t$}
  \STATE $E_t \gets f_\theta(X_t, A)$ \COMMENT{centralised, one row per gNB}
  \STATE for each pending request at gNB $i$, slice $k$, pick $a \gets \arg\max_a Q_{\phi_k}(E_t[i])[a]$, $\epsilon$-greedy \COMMENT{decentralised, own row only}
  \STATE step env, store $(X_t, X_{t+1}, \{(i,k,a)\}, r_t, \text{done})$ in $\mathcal{D}$
  \IF{$|\mathcal{D}| \geq$ warmup}
    \STATE sample minibatch $B$; $E_B \gets f_\theta(X_B,A)$, $E'_B \gets f_{\theta^-}(X'_B,A)$
    \STATE per request, $y \gets r + \gamma(1{-}\text{done})\max_{a'} Q_{\phi_k^-}(E'_B[i])[a']$
    \STATE $\mathcal{L} \gets$ mean per-request Huber loss, never summed per step; update $\theta,\{\phi_k\}$, clip grad-norm 1.0
  \ENDIF
  \STATE every $C$ episodes, $\theta^-\gets\theta$, $\phi_k^-\gets\phi_k$
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Federated Training with Differential Privacy}
Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}b shows the federated variant, in which each
gNB becomes a federated-learning client holding a private copy of the
same network and training only on its own requests. Transitions are
partitioned by owning agent, so no client sees another's raw admission
data, while its GAT encoder still consumes the full joint node-feature
snapshot. Every $R$ local steps the server averages all clients' online
weights by FedAvg~\cite{fedavg} and broadcasts the result back. We also
implement FedProx~\cite{fedprox}, which adds a proximal term pulling
local weights toward the last broadcast global model. Swept at $\mu \in
\{0.01, 0.1, 1.0\}$ under both homogeneous and heterogeneous per-gNB
load, FedProx earns no measurable dividend at any strength, since
per-round client drift is negligible relative to the loss scale.

Differential privacy, a formal guarantee that one client's training data
cannot be inferred from the shared model, is applied as a standard
DP-SGD~\cite{dpsgd} step. Every client's gradient is clipped to a fixed
norm $C_{\text{dp}}$ and, when enabled, perturbed with Gaussian noise
scaled by a noise multiplier $\sigma$ before that client's optimiser
step. We report $\sigma$ as the primary privacy parameter alongside a
formal $(\varepsilon,\delta)$ bound via zero-concentrated differential
privacy~\cite{zcdp}, where $\varepsilon$ and $\delta$ are the standard
privacy-loss and failure-probability parameters. Composing the Gaussian
mechanism over $m$ per-client gradient releases gives the zCDP parameter
$\rho$ and the corresponding bound
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:zcdp}
\rho=\frac{m}{2\sigma^{2}},\qquad
\varepsilon(\delta)=\rho+2\sqrt{\rho\ln(1/\delta)}.
\end{equation}
Clipping is applied identically whether or not noise is added, so a
$\sigma{=}0$ run and a $\sigma{>}0$ run differ only in whether noise is
injected, the isolation the privacy-cost comparison in
Section~\ref{sec:results-m3} depends on. Algorithm~\ref{alg:fed-dp}
states one federated round, with proximal weight $\mu$ the FedProx
strength swept above.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Federated GAT-CTDE round with DP-SGD}
\label{alg:fed-dp}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE $N$ clients with local nets $(\theta_i,\{\phi_{i,k}\})$, global snapshot $\bar\theta$, local steps $R$, clip norm $C_{\text{dp}}$, noise $\sigma$, FedProx weight $\mu$
\FOR{each round}
  \FOR{$R$ local steps, each client $i$ independently}
    \STATE $\mathcal{L}_i \gets$ per-request Huber TD loss of Alg.~\ref{alg:gat-ctde} on $i$'s own requests $+\ \frac{\mu}{2}\lVert \theta_i - \bar\theta \rVert^2$
    \STATE $g_i \gets \text{clip}(\nabla\mathcal{L}_i,\,C_{\text{dp}})$, then $g_i \gets g_i + \mathcal{N}(0,(\sigma C_{\text{dp}})^2 I)$ if $\sigma>0$ \COMMENT{same clip isolates privacy cost}
    \STATE $(\theta_i,\phi_i) \gets (\theta_i,\phi_i) - \eta\,g_i$
  \ENDFOR
  \STATE $\bar\theta,\bar\phi \gets \frac{1}{N}\sum_i (\theta_i,\phi_i)$ and broadcast to every client \COMMENT{FedAvg}
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
Both campaigns use 300 training and 50 evaluation episodes per seed. The
centralised campaign uses a fixed 30-seed list, seeds 900--929, across
three arms, namely GAT-CTDE, a single-agent DQN baseline run against the
flattened joint state, and an independent-DQN ablation. An ablation is a
deliberately reduced variant with one component removed to isolate its
effect, here one per-gNB DQN with no parameter sharing and no topology
information, which isolates the contribution of the shared encoder and
centralised training. All three arms use matched hyperparameters, with
discount factor $\gamma=0.95$ and per-episode epsilon decay. The
federated campaign uses the first 10 of these seeds and a five-level
noise sweep $\sigma \in \{0, 0.5, 1, 2, 4\}$, so its $\sigma{=}0$ level
is directly comparable to the same 10 centralised seeds. All confidence
intervals are 95\% bootstrap percentile intervals over 10{,}000
resamples, and paired comparisons use the Wilcoxon signed-rank test.

\subsection{Evaluation Safeguards}
\label{sec:collapse}
Two safeguards make the results below trustworthy, and
Table~\ref{tab:safeguards} summarises the four pipeline failures they
caught, each of which a standard pipeline would have surfaced as a clean
result. The first safeguard is a correctness-aware metric pair computed
from data every run already logs. Mean reward per step is the actual
training objective, and block precision is the fraction of blocked
requests correctly targeting mMTC, the only slice the reward calibration
ever makes reject-optimal. Writing $b_{\text{mMTC}}$ and $b$ for the
mMTC-targeted and total blocks over an evaluation,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:precision}
\pi = \frac{b_{\text{mMTC}}}{b}, \quad b>0,\qquad \text{undefined for } b=0,
\end{equation}
so a seed that never blocks is excluded rather than scored as a success.

\label{sec:correctness-metrics}
These are needed because \texttt{sla\_compliance\_all\_slices} scores
worse when the policy becomes more correct, since blocking mMTC under
congestion never recovers mMTC's own SLA margin in this stress regime.
Re-checking every paired comparison in this paper against both metrics,
the correctness-aware pair and compliance disagree on direction or
significance in 44 of 49 comparisons, 89.8\%, confirmed at 21 of 24 on
a disjoint supplementary seed range. Every number in this paper that
matters is therefore reported through the correctness-aware pair.

The second safeguard is a reproduction-versus-replication discipline,
where every headline comparison is recomputed on an independent,
disjoint seed sample drawn after the first and both are reported
together. Reproduction re-runs the same seeds, whereas replication draws
fresh ones, so only replication can catch a result that is an artifact
of one seed sample. Applied to the disruption campaign in
Section~\ref{sec:results-m4}, it corrected an early reading that
independent DQN was immune to agent churn, which the committed sample
alone had made look confirmed. The remaining two failures were a logging
bug that had stacked three runs' evaluation episodes in each seed's log,
cleared and re-evaluated against the already-trained checkpoints with no
retraining, and a live scheduler-floor misconfiguration resolved in
Section~\ref{sec:results-m8}.

\subsection{Disruption}
\label{sec:results-m4}
We evaluate every arm's frozen checkpoint, with no retraining, against
three mid-episode disruptions injected at evaluation time. 'gNB dropout'
forces a gNB's requests to reject and zeroes its row in the joint
feature matrix for a window. 'Demand spike' is a transient arrival-rate
multiplier external to the policy. 'Agent churn' substitutes a gNB's
decisions with a fresh, never-trained copy of its own policy class for a
window, leaving other agents untouched, and applies to every genuinely
multi-agent arm but not single-agent DQN. Severity is the window
duration, 10\%, 30\%, or 60\% of the episode, for dropout and churn, and
the arrival-rate multiplier, 2$\times$, 4$\times$, or 8$\times$, for
spike. Since raw reward inflates mechanically with request volume, we
report the volume-normalised mean reward $\bar r$ over an episode of $T$
steps, dividing each step reward $r_t$ by its own pending-request count
$n_t$ before averaging,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:norm}
\bar r \;=\; \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{r_t}{\max(n_t,\,1)}.
\end{equation}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig5_m4_disruption.pdf}
\caption{M4 disruption campaign, 10 seeds per arm. a, b, Normalised
reward cost versus severity for dropout and churn, accelerating with
severity. c, Block precision versus demand-spike multiplier, flat and
near-ceiling.}
\label{fig:m4-disruption}
\end{figure}

Every dropout and churn cost accelerates with severity rather than
growing proportionally, with Wilcoxon $p\leq0.03$ and 9 or 10 of 10
seeds hurt in every case. On the committed sample GAT-CTDE's dropout cost
rises 0.165, 0.601, 1.710 across the three severities and its churn cost
0.059, 0.200, 0.529, with every other arm showing the same accelerating
shape. Demand spike does not corrupt decision quality on the
volume-invariant block-precision metric in Fig.~\ref{fig:m4-disruption}c,
so its apparent per-step reward gain is a denominator artifact rather
than better decisions.

Independent DQN's churn cost needs both seed samples to state honestly.
The committed sample is small and not conventionally significant, which
an earlier pass read as independent DQN being immune to churn, since it
never attends to another agent's features. A disjoint independent-seed
replication is significant at every severity and larger than the
coordinated arms', its cost rising 0.097, 0.657, 1.223 against the
committed 0.000, 0.168, 0.640, so the corrected reading is that churn
always hurts and treating a borderline $n{=}10$ result as a confirmed
property was the error. This is the one exception among four
cross-section headline comparisons checked against an independent sample.
The other three replicate with overlapping intervals, namely GAT-CTDE
over independent DQN on reward at +1.461 then +0.767 with $p<0.0001$ in
both, GAT-CTDE over single-agent at +0.195 then +0.445, and a federation
cost negligible in both.

\subsection{Cluster-Size Scaling}
\label{sec:results-m6}
We extend the cluster from $N{=}3$ to $N \in \{7, 19\}$ under three
adjacency structures, fully-connected, ring, and hex-grid, scaling
synthetic arrivals with $N$ to hold per-gNB offered load roughly
constant. GAT-CTDE is the only arm whose Q-head consumes this adjacency,
so independent DQN and single-agent DQN serve as a topology-invariance
check. We normalise reward per gNB as in Eq.~\ref{eq:norm} and report
block precision alongside, since compliance is OR-aggregated across gNBs
and trends to zero as $N$ grows regardless of policy quality. The
primary sample is 12 seeds at $N{=}19$ across all topologies and arms,
with independent replication seeds for generalisation.

At $N{=}19$ the paired per-gNB reward margin is not significant for any
topology, which is mechanically expected since single-agent DQN's
always-accept collapse collects the service term on every request while
GAT-CTDE pays a real cost each time it correctly rejects mMTC. The
advantage therefore lives in collapse resistance and block precision.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig6_m6_topology.pdf}
\caption{$N{=}19$ cluster, per-seed evidence. a, Collapse rate pooled
across topologies, single-agent DQN total and GAT-CTDE converging to
35.4\% across 64 seeds. b, Block precision by topology for non-collapsed
seeds.}
\label{fig:m6-topology}

Single-agent DQN collapses totally at $N{=}19$, 15 of 15 cells combined
across both samples and every topology, confirmed at the
individual-decision level by a direct Q-value probe. GAT-CTDE collapses
only partially, and independent DQN never fully collapses but sits in a
narrow, mediocre 0.29--0.49 block-precision band identical across
topologies, as expected for an arm that never reads adjacency.

GAT-CTDE's collapse rate converges, across 64 independently-drawn seeds
pooling 68 of 192 cells, to
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:collapse}
\hat p_{\text{collapse}} = 35.4\%,\qquad \text{95\% CI } [25.5\%,\;45.8\%],
\end{equation}
a single seed-level-bootstrapped estimate rather than either of two
smaller disagreeing samples. A properly powered 12-seed resample at
$N{=}7$ under the identical stress environment, with a disjoint 12-seed
replication, puts GAT-CTDE at 41.7\% and 58.3\% collapse across the two
samples, statistically indistinguishable from Eq.~\ref{eq:collapse}, so
its collapse-proneness is present from $N{=}7$ onward rather than emerging
only at large cluster size. Over the same two samples single-agent DQN
collapses at 91.7\% and 75.0\% against its earlier three-seed pilot's
0\%, the clearest single result in this extension, and independent DQN at
0.0\% and 16.7\%, confirming it collapses rarely rather than never.

\subsection{Live Deployment}
\label{sec:results-m8}
This section reports what the real OAI testbed of Fig.~\ref{fig:testbed},
introduced in Section~\ref{sec:system-model}, can and cannot establish
about the offline-trained policy, using a single-agent-DQN checkpoint,
seed 900, against a real gNB, real UEs, and real per-slice User Datagram
Protocol (UDP) traffic at native load of 1 UE per slice and double native
load of 2 UEs per slice.

A live capacity probe at a deliberately wide-open ceiling found all three
slices serve essentially 100\% of offered demand with 0.000\% loss at
both loads. The frozen checkpoint's decision logic executes correctly end
to end, with real traffic through the real E2 loop, real accept and
reject decisions, and its characteristic low-priority-only blocking of
mMTC reproducing at both loads. This establishes deployability. Since the
rig never reaches scarcity, the pattern is equally consistent with the
policy's trained priority bias, so admission-control-under-contention is
claimed only in the disclosed offline stress regime, not live. An early
scheduler-configuration issue, where \texttt{saclb\_live.yaml}'s
per-slice PRB-ratio ranges converted to raw grants below the gNB
scheduler's hard 5-PRB minimum, was fixed at its root, after which direct
instrumentation confirmed zero below-floor grants across more than
100{,}000 readings. A separate unit-mismatch in the live SLA-margin
diagnostic is fixed by converting backlog to a queueing-delay estimate
via Little's Law against each slice's own latency budget.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We set out to test whether a standard evaluation metric and a live
testbed's evidentiary reach can be trusted in multi-agent,
privacy-constrained, disruption-prone slice admission control. A
correctness-aware metric pair, a reproduction-versus-replication
discipline, and direct live instrumentation together surfaced four
pipeline failures a standard pipeline would have reported as clean
results, and every headline number here is reported through those
safeguards.

On the metric that reflects the training objective, GAT-CTDE beats an
independent-learner ablation decisively, though its edge over the
single-agent baseline is small and not decisive at this sample size.
Federation adds no measurable reward cost, differential privacy is
costly only past a real precision threshold, and disruption cost
accelerates with severity for every arm. Scaling to 19 gNBs, single-agent
DQN collapses totally while GAT-CTDE remains only partially
collapse-prone, converging across 64 seeds on a 35.4\% rate with 95\% CI
[25.5\%, 45.8\%] already present at 7 gNBs. A live single-gNB deployment
confirms the offline-trained logic executes correctly on real hardware,
establishing deployability, with admission-control-under-contention
claimed only in the disclosed offline stress regime since this rig
cannot yet reach genuine per-slice scarcity.

The main limits follow from that scope. Every multi-gNB, federated, and
disruption result is offline, the stress environment is deliberately
harder than any load this rig reaches, the graph models only the gNB
cluster with no edge-compute dimension, and the federated setting is a
single-operator privacy scenario. The broader lesson is that evaluation
in these settings needs metrics tied to the training objective,
architectural claims need an independent seed sample before they are
trusted, and a live testbed's reach must be checked rather than assumed.
